How to use it

Start from a level page. It lists that level's kanji ordered by how often they appear in print, which is the order worth learning them in — the first fifty kanji of any level do far more work than the last fifty.

On a kanji page, read the common words before the readings. A reading learned on its own tends not to stick; a reading learned inside a word you will meet does.

Where the data comes from

Readings, radicals and frequency ranks come from KANJIDIC2, vocabulary from JMdict, example sentences from the Tanaka corpus, and stroke order from KanjiVG. Level membership is cross-checked against two public reference lists.

There has been no official JLPT kanji list since 2010, so every list is a reconstruction. The two used here differ only by omission — one carries 73 kanji the other leaves out — and never place the same kanji at different levels. See Sources for the numbers and Credits for licensing.
